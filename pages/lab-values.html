Organ-specific markers

Amylase vs lipase — timing is the answer

Amylase rises 3–6 h, peaks 20–30 h, normalises in 2–3 days. Lipase follows amylase but stays up to 14 days. Late presentation → lipase is your marker.

Acute pancreatitis: the classic picture

Cullen sign (periumbilical) and Grey Turner sign (flank) = retroperitoneal bleeding. Late, severe findings.

Nine physical exam signs worth memorising

Confirms collateral ulnar flow. Colour returns in 5–7 seconds = pass. No return in 10 = do not stick.

Cardiac tracing

PQRST — what each wave actually means

P = atrial depolarisation · QRS = ventricular depolarisation · T = ventricular repolarisation. Everything on an ECG report refers back to this.

Which leads = which coronary artery

II, III, aVF = inferior (RCA) · V1–V4 = anterior (LAD) · I, aVL, V5–V6 = lateral (LCx). ST elevation location tells you which vessel is blocked.

GI imaging & pathology

The nine abdominal regions and what lives in each

When a report says "epigastric" or "right iliac", this is the map. Pain location narrows the test list fast.

Upper GI landmarks the scope passes

Esophagus → LES → cardia → fundus → body → antrum → pylorus → duodenum. EGD visits all of them.

What the scope actually sees

Normal colonic mucosa (left) vs ulcerative colitis (right): loss of vascular pattern, friability, granularity.

Crohn disease findings on scope

Inflammation → stenosis → distended ileum → fistula. This is what "abnormal findings" means in words.

Gastric vs duodenal ulcer

The classic EGD finding. Location matters: gastric ulcers get biopsied to rule out cancer.

Three flavours of gastritis

Autoimmune, bacterial (H. pylori) and chemical — distinguished on biopsy, not on sight.

Peritonitis

The thing you are watching for after a perforation. Rigid abdomen, rebound tenderness, fever, tachycardia.

Intussusception

Bowel telescoping into itself — one of the things a scope or contrast study is hunting for.

A fistula, close up

An abnormal tract between two surfaces — a complication colonoscopy and manometry both look for.

Small bowel obstruction — presentation to management

Colicky pain, no flatus, hyperactive→absent bowel sounds. Diagnosis is imaging, not endoscopy.

Ileus vs small bowel obstruction

Bowel sounds are the tell: quiet/absent in ileus, high-pitched then absent in obstruction.

Normal vs paralytic ileus on plain film

Gas-filled loops of both large and small intestine.

Obstruction on abdominal X-ray

Dilated loops with air-fluid levels.

Volvulus — the "corkscrew" sign

Contrast study finding. A twisted bowel is a surgical emergency, not a scope candidate.

Pyloric stenosis

Projectile vomiting in an infant. Upper GI study territory.

Decode any procedure name in seconds

-scopy = look inside · -ectomy = remove · -ostomy = new opening · -otomy = cut into · -centesis = puncture to drain. Half this chapter decodes itself.
